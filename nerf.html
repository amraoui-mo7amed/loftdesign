Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF)

Generating Photorealistic 3D Views from 2D Photos & Videos

1. What is NeRF?

NeRF stands for Neural Radiance Field. It is an AI technology introduced in 2020 that takes a collection of 2D images (or a video captured around an object) and constructs a fully navigable, continuous 3D digital scene.

Unlike traditional 3D graphics that rely on 3D meshes made of triangles (polygons), NeRF stores the entire scene inside a Neural Network.

2. Interactive Demo: Ray Marching Concept

NeRF works by shooting rays from the camera into 3D space and querying the AI model along the ray to calculate color and density.

3. How It Works (Step-by-Step)

  1. Capture Images: You take 50–200 photos or a short video from different angles around an object or scene.
  2. Camera Position Estimation: Algorithms calculate the exact location (x, y, z) and orientation of the camera for each shot.
  3. Ray Marching: For every pixel in an image, a virtual ray is cast through 3D space.
  4. 5D Input Vector: Points along the ray are passed to a neural network using 5 inputs:
    • Position in space: (x, y, z)
    • Viewing direction: (θ, φ)
  5. Neural Network Output: The AI outputs 2 things for every point:
    • RGB Color: What color is at this point?
    • Volume Density (σ): Is this point solid, semi-transparent, or empty air?
  6. Volume Rendering: The colors and densities along the ray are accumulated to render the final 2D pixel color.

4. NeRF vs. Traditional 3D Modeling

Traditional 3D (Photogrammetry)

  • Uses explicit geometry (polygons, vertices, meshes).
  • Struggles with reflection, transparent glass, and fine details like hair.
  • Fast rendering on standard game engines.

NeRF (Neural Fields)

  • Stores geometry implicitly inside AI weight parameters.
  • Captures photorealistic reflections, glass, and intricate lighting seamlessly.
  • Historically required heavy GPU processing to render views.

5. The Evolution: Gaussian Splatting

While original NeRFs offered unmatched visual realism, they were slow to train and render. In 2023, 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) emerged as the next evolution:

  • Instead of querying a neural network for every point along a ray, it represents scenes as millions of tiny 3D "ellipsoids" (Gaussians).
  • Allows real-time rendering at 60+ FPS with zero quality loss.
